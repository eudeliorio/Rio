MXN liquidity by the hour: why Friday at 5pm costs more, and what a treasury can do about it

USD/MXN is most liquid in the New York morning and thinnest from 5pm to 7pm New York time and over the weekend, when the interbank market is closed. Converting in those hours costs more with any provider. With Rio, the wider spread shows in the quote before you trade, and pesos still settle over SPEI in one to two minutes.

You can trade the peso at any hour. You cannot trade it well at any hour. The gap between those two sentences is a cost most treasuries pay every week without seeing it.

The peso trades 24/7. The people who price it work New York hours.

The rail that moves pesos, SPEI, never closes either. So the demand for pesos on a Saturday is real. The supply of pesos at a fair price on a Saturday is not. If any part of your flow into or out of Mexico trades outside US working hours, this is about what that costs, and how to stop paying it.

Why is the peso not equally liquid at every hour?

FX has no closing bell, but liquidity is not about the clock. It is about who is at their desk. The peso is a dollar market: 98% of it trades against USD, 82% of it outside Mexico, mostly in New York and London. Follow one week, New York time.

  1. Sunday, 5pm

    Asia opens. The interbank market comes back to life after the weekend. Thin, but there is a live price again.

  2. Monday to Friday, 8am to noon

    London and New York are both working. The most desks are quoting pesos. This is where the market is deepest and spreads are tightest. If you can choose when to trade, choose here.

  3. Weekdays, noon to 5pm

    London goes home. New York carries the peso alone. Still liquid, less so each hour.

  4. Weekdays, 5pm to 7pm

    The seam. New York has closed and Tokyo has not opened. Neither side of the world is fully at work. This is the thinnest stretch of the working day, and the one where surprise news moves the price most, because there is nobody to absorb it.

  5. Friday 5pm to Sunday 5pm

    The interbank market is closed. There is no live USD/MXN price at all. Whoever quotes you pesos on a Saturday is taking a view on where the market will reopen and holding that risk until it does.

None of this is special to Mexico. What is special to Mexico is the peso rail underneath it. SPEI runs 24 hours a day and settles in seconds, so the payment side of your business never stops. Only the pricing side does.

How much it costs to trade pesos off-hours

A wider spread. Market makers price liquidity by risk. Fewer players to take the other side, harder to hedge, so the spread opens to compensate. The same USD 2 million ticket that clears tight at 10am Tuesday clears wider at 6pm Friday.

A worse chance of getting filled. In thin markets the price you see and the price you get drift apart more often. On venues with last look, the practice of rejecting a trade after you accepted it, rejections rise with volatility, and volatility rises when depth falls. So the trade you most need done is the one most likely to be bounced and re-quoted worse.

A calendar you did not choose. Treasuries that understand the first two start batching conversions into the liquid window. Reasonable, except that now the market sets the payment schedule instead of the business. A remittance company should not have to think about New York hours to decide when to fund Saturday payouts.

Where does the dollar-peso rate come from on weekends?

A provider that quotes on Saturday is not being generous or greedy. It is carrying rate risk for you for up to two days and charging for it. That risk is real and it belongs in the price.

The honest way to handle it is not to pretend the Saturday price is the Tuesday price. It is to show the weekend price as what it is, before you trade, so you can decide whether the payout has to go now or can wait for the Sunday open.

How Rio quotes and settles pesos 24/7, including weekends and holidays

Rio quotes and settles USDC and USDT against MXN 24 hours a day, seven days a week, including Mexican and US holidays. Three things about how that works.

The quote tells you what time it is. Every quote is an all-in price against the live market rate, firm for the full amount for about ten seconds, with the spread shown separately. Off-hours and on weekends that spread is wider, because the market is thinner, and you see it in the quote before you accept, not on the statement after. No last look, so a quote you accept is a trade that executes.

Settlement does not wait for Monday. Pesos land over SPEI in one to two minutes at any hour. The interbank market being closed affects the price of the conversion, not the delivery of the pesos.

You can skip the thin hours entirely. If you know on Friday morning what you will pay out on Saturday, lock the rate with a forward for value on Saturday. If your inflows are large and continuous, work them through the liquid window with a TWAP instead of crossing a lump at 6pm. If you need to pay out before your dollars arrive, Rio's overdraft lets you fund the payout in the liquid window and settle your side up to three days later.

One more. When Rio's own clients have flow in both directions, one selling pesos while another buys, the desk can match them and quote well inside the market spread for that moment. Those axe alerts are worth most exactly when the outside market is thinnest.

What to ask your FX provider about off-hours spreads

Not "what is your spread." Ask: "what was my spread at 6pm last Friday?" If the answer is the same number as Tuesday at 10am, one of two things is true. Either the provider is eating the off-hours cost, which will not last, or the Tuesday number was never as tight as it looked. Piece 02 in this series shows how to measure it yourself.

FAQ

Does the FX market ever actually close?

The interbank market does. Trading runs continuously from the Sunday evening open in Asia to the Friday evening close in New York, each region handing off to the next. Over the weekend there is effectively no interbank trading and no live USD/MXN price. Desks like Rio keep quoting through it, but the price reflects the risk of holding the position until the market reopens.

Why is the peso cheaper to trade at some hours than others?

Because the cost of converting a currency depends on how many market makers are competing to quote it at that moment. In the New York morning, with London still open, the most desks are active in pesos and the spread is tightest. After the US close, fewer desks quote, each carries more risk, and the price shows it.

What is the worst time of day to trade pesos?

Roughly 5pm to 7pm New York time, after the US close and before Tokyo opens. It is also when surprise news moves the price most, because there are fewer players to absorb it. For a currency as tied to the dollar as the peso, the US close matters more than it does for a pair with a deep Asian market.

Can I avoid weekend and holiday pricing entirely?

Often. If the weekend payout is predictable, lock the rate with a forward during the liquid window for value on the weekend. If the dollars will not arrive until Monday, use an overdraft to fund the payout on Friday and settle up to three days later. If the flow is continuous, work it through the liquid window with a TWAP. Holidays in Mexico or the US behave like weekends for pricing. What you cannot avoid is an unplanned Saturday conversion, and for that the only protection is a provider that shows you the weekend price before you accept it.

Does SPEI run on weekends?

Yes. SPEI operates 24 hours a day, every day, with scheduled maintenance windows that Banco de México announces in advance. Pesos sent over SPEI on a Saturday credit in seconds. The limit on weekend FX is the price of the conversion, not the rail.
